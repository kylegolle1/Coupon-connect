# Market study summary (2026-11-22)

Condensed from kyle's doc "Existing coupon apps: market study"
(https://claude.ai/artifact/83dsuH9uaNVL1uqTkcdnNb). The full doc has every source link.

## Headline
Parts of the idea exist, but no app lets many big chains post their own coupons as barcodes
you scan at checkout. Shoppers are tired of juggling apps and digital-only deals. The hard
part is getting chains to share coupons, not building the app.

## Existing apps
| App | What it does | Reach | Rating |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flipp | Weekly ads + some coupons from 2,000+ stores | claims 100M shoppers | 4.8 (538K App Store ratings) |

## What shoppers complain about
2026 Krazy Coupon Lady survey of 3,750 of its app users (leans toward keen couponers):
- 36% coupon to afford essentials; 37% to buy things otherwise out of budget.
- Top frustrations: expired or broken coupons 26%, products rarely couponed 22%,
  stacking/limit rules 19%, too many apps 17%, takes too long 16%.
- 31% of non-couponers say it's too complicated.

Digital-only coupons face backlash: they shut out seniors and lower-income shoppers.
San Diego banned digital-only grocery coupons; bills filed in IL, RI, PA. Kroger now prints
weekly digital deals on paper. Other complaints: cluttered store apps, weak phone signal
in stores, privacy.

## What it means for us
- One tap to checkout, no clipping. Always current (auto-remove expired: answers the 26%).
- Big text, simple layout, works offline (weak in-store signal, older shoppers).
- Sort by biggest savings on essentials.

## Big hurdles
1. Chains want shoppers in their own apps; start with regional grocers or brands.
2. Register systems must accept the barcode; many store coupons are tied to loyalty accounts.
3. Brand coupons are reimbursed through clearinghouses (e.g. Inmar), about 4–6 weeks.
4. Big rivals (Ibotta, Flipp) could copy the feature.
5. Revenue likely from stores/brands per redemption or placement; free for shoppers.
